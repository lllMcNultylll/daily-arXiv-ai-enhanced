*Miguel Rojas-Quesada,Lyndsay Fletcher,Hugh Hudson,Sargam M. Mulay,Paulo J. A. Simoes*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: 本文通过分析太阳耀斑中5.2微米和8.2微米两个中红外波段的发射时间延迟（8.2微米比5.2微米早0.3-0.45秒），结合RADYN辐射流体动力学模型，发现该延迟主要由耀斑诱导电离引起的 opacity 变化导致，为研究耀斑能量沉积和色球层加热提供了新手段。

Motivation: 中红外发射与太阳耀斑中能量注入之间存在强相关性，可用于探测色球层中的能量沉积过程。然而，太阳中红外观测相对较新且数据稀缺，此前研究仅发现空间分辨足点间的中红外发射时间延迟，但同足点不同波长间的延迟尚未被分析，本文旨在填补这一空白。

Method: 采用局部互相关函数分析5.2微米和8.2微米两个中红外波段在同一耀斑足点的发射时间延迟；利用RADYN辐射流体动力学代码模拟耀斑能量注入过程，估计理论红外发射延迟，并与观测结果对比。

Result: 首次发现8.2微米通道比5.2微米通道早0.3-0.45秒达到峰值；理论延迟值落在观测范围之内；opacity变化（主要由耀斑诱导电离引起）解释了波长依赖的时间偏移；较长波长的发射峰值与能量注入峰值之间的延迟更小。

Conclusion: 该结果为理解耀斑能量沉积如何影响色球层提供了新见解；未来更高时间分辨率的观测可利用这些时间延迟更全面地表征耀斑能量沉积动力学，为研究太阳大气加热和能量输运过程开辟新途径。

Abstract: The strong correlation between energy injection and mid-infrared (mid-IR) emission observed during solar flares can be used to probe energy deposition throughout the chromosphere, since the IR tracks prompt flare-induced changes in electron density. Despite its diagnostic value, solar mid-IR observations are relatively recent, with sporadic campaigns over the last decade resulting in only a few recorded flares. Earlier studies found time lags between mid-IR emissions from spatially resolved footpoints, offering clues about flare energy transport. Building on this, we analyse the time lags between emissions at two wavelengths (5.2 micrometers and 8.2 micrometers) for each footpoint. Using a local cross-correlation function, we show for the first time that the 8.2 micrometers emission channel peaks 0.3 s-0.45 s before the 5.2 micrometer channel. We investigate the origin of this lag, obtaining infrared emission estimates using results from the RADYN radiation hydrodynamics code. The theoretical lag values fall within the range of the observed ones. Variations in opacity-primarily due to flare-induced ionization-explain the wavelength-dependent temporal shift between emission maxima. In particular, longer wavelengths exhibit a smaller lag between the peak of energy injection and peak of intensity. These results contribute to a better understanding of how energy deposition during a flare affects the chromospheric layers of the atmosphere. Future observations with higher temporal resolution could exploit measurements of these time lags to more fully characterize the dynamics of energy deposition during solar flares, opening a new avenue for studying heating and energy transport processes in the solar atmosphere.
