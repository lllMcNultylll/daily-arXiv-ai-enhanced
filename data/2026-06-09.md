Method: 使用ALMA Band 3（100 GHz）和Band 6（243 GHz）连续谱观测，结合VLA 5-22.5 GHz数据，构建宽带射电-毫米波谱指数，通过频率依赖的团块诊断分析恒星风径向结构，并利用毫米波通量密度推导团块标度的质量损失率。

Result: 探测到23颗大质量恒星；Wolf-Rayet星谱指数集中在α≈0.7-0.8（热自由-自由辐射），部分O型星表现为平坦或负谱指数（非热同步辐射）；WN星质量损失率log(Ṁ)≈ -4.1至-4.9，O型超/超超巨星≈ -4.9至-5.4；恒星风团块结构随半径增大而减弱。

Conclusion: 射电-毫米波联合观测能有效约束大质量恒星的质量损失和风结构；Arches星团中的大质量恒星存在显著的内层风团块非均匀性，支持结构化风模型；结果有助于理解极端星团环境中的恒星反馈。

Abstract: We present the first Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) Band 3 (100 GHz) and Band 6 (243 GHz) continuum observations of the Arches cluster, one of the youngest and most massive stellar clusters in the Milky Way. We detect and characterise millimetre emission from 23 massive stars, including WN7-9h Wolf-Rayet stars, O-type supergiants and hypergiants. By combining our ALMA measurements with archival Very Large Array data spanning 5-22.5 GHz, we derive broadband radio-millimetre spectral indices and investigate the radial structure of stellar winds through frequency-dependent clumping diagnostics. The majority of Wolf-Rayet stars exhibit spectral indices clustered around $α\approx 0.7-0.8$, consistent with predominantly thermal free-free emission from dense, partially optically thick winds. In contrast, several O-type stars show flat or negative broadband spectral indices, indicative of non-thermal synchrotron emission likely associated with colliding-wind binaries. Using millimetre flux densities, we derive clumping-scaled mass-loss rates spanning $\log(\dot{M}/\mathrm{M}_{\odot}\,\text{yr}^{-1})\approx-4.1$ to $-4.9$ for the WN stars and $-4.9$ to $-5.4$ for the O super-/hypergiants, consistent with expectations for luminous massive stars in the Galactic Centre environment. We find significant evidence of structured wind clumping at millimetre wavelengths that generally decreases with increasing radius, supporting structured wind models with strong inner-wind inhomogeneities. These results demonstrate the power of combined radio-millimetre observations for constraining mass-loss and wind structure in massive stars, and provide new insight into stellar feedback in extreme cluster environments.
