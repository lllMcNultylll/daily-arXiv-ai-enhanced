We compile a catalog of 974 CSs of thickness in the range of few to 90 ion inertial lengths, of which 53 contain an ion reconnection jet. By comparing these results with previous studies we estimate that the abundance of CSs and of Reconnecting CSs evolves with heliocentric distance ($r$) as $r^{-0.9}$ and $r^{-1.5}$, respectively. We estimate that magnetic reconnection dissipates $ε_{rec}\sim 30 \hspace{5pt} \text{J} \text{kg}^{-1} \text{s}^{-1},$ in the $\sim 1 - 40$ $δ_i$ scale-range, by assessing the amount of plasma that interacts with a reconnection exhaust. We show how the dissipation of turbulence energy cascade by magnetic reconnection at scales smaller than 90 $δ_i$ is more effective closer to the Sun, and decays as $r^{-3.6}$. The current density of Alfvénic CSs thicker than the ion inertial length presents scale invariance, obeying $J_0/J_A=0.9(λ/2δ_i)^{-0.71}$, suggesting that CSs generated locally from solar wind turbulence.
