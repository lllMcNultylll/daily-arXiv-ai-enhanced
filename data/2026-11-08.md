Abstract: Long secondary periods (LSPs), observed in a substantial fraction of luminous red giants, remain one of the longstanding problems in red giant variability. The stacked light and RV curves of the LSP sample also show remarkable homogeneity and a coherent phase relation. In the binary interpretation of LSPs, the observed phase offset requires the obscuring material to be shifted in orbital phase with respect to the companion, consistent with models in which a low-mass companion produces a dusty wake in the wind of the red giant.
